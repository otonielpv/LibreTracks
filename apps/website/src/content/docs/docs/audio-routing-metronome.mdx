Live, the usual thing is to separate what the audience hears from what the
band hears: the **music** goes to the sound desk and the **click** and the
**guide** go only to the musicians' in-ears. LibreTracks does it by sending
each track to a different output of your audio interface.

If you are after a complete plan, from the stems to the desk, with levels and
a soundcheck, see the guide
[How to route multitrack audio live](/guides/multitrack-audio-routing/).

## 1. Choose the audio interface

In **Settings › Audio** choose the audio system and the device. For live use,
a dedicated interface is better than the computer's sound card. What each
setting means is in [Settings › Audio](/docs/interface/settings/#audio).

## 2. Enable the outputs

In the same tab, **Hardware outputs** shows every channel your interface
really has: two on a stereo interface, eight, sixteen or thirty-two on USB
interfaces and digital desks.

![Hardware outputs in Settings › Audio](/guide/desktop-en/settings-audio-2.png)

1. Tick the channels you are going to use (**Select all** and **Clear** help).
2. Press **Apply**. The interface reopens just once with all the changes;
   **Discard** leaves things as they were.

Only the ticked channels show up afterwards as outputs on the tracks.

## 3. Send each track to its output

On each track's header, the output selector:

![A track's output selector](/guide/desktop-en/track-route-list.png)

- **Master**: the main output (the first two channels). It goes through the
  song's master.
- **Out 3**, **Out 4**…: one physical channel, in mono.
- **Out 3/4**…: a pair of channels, in stereo.
- **Inherited (Folder)**: only on tracks inside a folder. The track goes out
  wherever its folder goes. When you put a track in a folder, it is set like
  this by itself.

:::tip[Tip: route the folder, not each track]
Put the click and the guide in a folder, send the folder to the in-ear output
and leave the tracks on *Inherited (Folder)*. If you change interface
tomorrow, you only touch one selector.
:::

A stereo track that goes to a mono output plays in full if you turn on **Sum
to mono** in its menu (right-click).

A typical split:

| What | Output |
| --- | --- |
| Music stems | Master (to the desk) |
| Click, count-ins, spoken guides | Out 3/4 (to the in-ears) |
| Metronome and voice guide | The same in-ear output |

## The metronome

LibreTracks has its own metronome: there is no need to import any click file.
It follows each song's [tempo and time signature](/docs/tasks/tempo/),
including changes mid-song.

- The **Click** button in the top bar turns it on and off.
- The **arrow** next to it opens its settings.

![The metronome settings](/guide/desktop-en/metronome-popover.png)

- **Enable metronome**: the same as the *Click* button.
- **Metronome output**: which output it goes to. Set it to the in-ear one, not
  Master.
- **Metronome volume**.
- **Accent enabled**: the first beat of each bar sounds different. Its
  **sound** and **pitch** are chosen separately.
- **Beat sound** and **Beat pitch**: the rest of the beats.
- **Subdivision**: eighths, triplets or sixteenths between beats, with their
  own sound, pitch and volume. Handy in slow ballads.

The metronome does not go through the song's master: turning down a song's
master doesn't turn down the click.

## The voice guide

The voice that announces the sections has its own output and volume, under the
arrow next to **Guide**. See [Voice guide](/docs/voice-guide/).
