A template keeps tracks, folders, colours and outputs. Besides creating
sessions, it creates **songs** and **organises the audio you drop**: drop
`Way Maker - EG 1.wav`, `Way Maker - Keys.wav`… into a song and each one lands
in its folder (Guitars, Keys…) by its name.

With no template picked, everything works exactly as before.

## Pick the template

In **Settings › General**, pick **Template for new songs**:

![Template for new songs](/guide/desktop-en/settings-song-template.png)

**Band (built in)** has Click, Guide, Drums, Bass, Guitars, Keys, Synths &
pads, Vocals and FX, with the names commercial multitracks use in English and
Spanish (EG, AG, Keys, BGV, FX…). Click and Guide come with **T** off, so they
do not go out of tune when you transpose. Your own session templates are listed too.

:::note
The built-in template sends Click and Guide to the master. If your in-ears use
another output, change their outputs and save your own template with **File ›
Save as template…**.
:::

## Build your own template

The built-in template is a starting point. If your band has a different
line-up, or you want Click and Guide on the in-ear output, build yours once and
every song comes out organised the same way.

### 1. Set up the folders and tracks

In an empty session (**File › New project**), create one folder per instrument
family and leave as loose tracks the parts that are always a single file.
Right-click a track header: **Insert Folder Track**, **Insert Track**,
**Indent Into Previous Folder** and **Select colour…**.

![The layout of a band template](/guide/desktop-en/template-structure.png)

1. A **folder** (Guitars): takes every guitar file, one per track, however
   many the multitrack has.
2. A **track** inside a folder (Click): takes a single file.

### 2. Route the outputs

Routing does go into the template, so this is the moment to decide what goes
to the desk and what goes to the in-ears. On each track's or folder's header,
the output selector:

![A track's output selector](/guide/desktop-en/track-route-list.png)

- Send the **folder** to its output and leave its tracks on **Inherited
  (Folder)**. The tracks the template creates inside that folder when you drop
  files go out there too.
- The usual setup: the Guides folder (Click and Guide) to the in-ear output,
  for example **Out 3/4**, and everything else to **Master**.
- If a stereo file goes to a mono output, turn on **Sum to mono**
  (right-click the track): it is saved too.

The outputs you see depend on your audio interface. How to turn them on is in
[Audio outputs and metronome](/docs/audio-routing-metronome/).

### 3. Give each one its rules

Rules are the words in a file name that send it to that track or folder.
Right-click the header › **Matching rules…**:

![Matching rules in the folder's menu](/guide/desktop-en/template-rules-menu.png)

Type the words separated by commas. Think of how the multitracks you use name
that part, in English and in Spanish:

![The rules of the Guitars folder](/guide/desktop-en/template-rules-dialog.png)

1. With `eg, ag, guitar, guitarra, gtr`, files such as `EG 1.wav`, `AG.wav`
   or `Rhythm Guitar.wav` land in Guitars.

Rules that work with most multitracks:

| Track or folder | Rules |
| --- | --- |
| Click | `click, metronome, metronomo` |
| Guide | `guide, guia, cues` |
| Drums | `drums, bateria, perc, loop` |
| Bass | `bass, bajo` |
| Guitars | `eg, ag, guitar, guitarra, gtr` |
| Keys | `keys, key, piano, synth, pad, organ` |
| Vocals | `alto, tenor, soprano, bgv, bgvs, choir, coro` |
| FX | `fx, efectos` |

### 4. Save it as a template

**File › Save as template…** and give it a name. It saves a `.lttemplate`
file.

![Save as template in the File menu](/guide/desktop-en/template-save-menu.png)

The template keeps each track's layout, not one night's mix:

- **Saved:** names, folders and their order, colours, outputs, **Sum to
  mono**, pan, **T** (follow transposition) and matching rules.
- **Back to its default:** volume (0 dB), and mute and solo (off).

Clips, songs, markers and tempo are not saved either.

:::tip[Turn off T on the Click and the Guide]
That way they do not go out of tune when you transpose a song with warp, and
every new song brings them already off. See [the T button](/docs/pitch-and-warp/).
:::

:::tip
A `.lttemplate` file works on any computer. Send it to another musician in the
band and they get the same layout.
:::

### 5. Pick it and drop a multitrack

In **Settings › General › Template for new songs**, pick yours (see
[Pick the template](#pick-the-template)). Then drag a multitrack's folder from
the [Library](/docs/tasks/library/) to the timeline, or drop its files: each
one goes to its place.

  caption="The 14 files of «Rey de Reyes» each land in their folder: Drums and Loop in Drums, EG 1-3 in Guitars, Alto and Tenor in Vocals."

The result: each folder with one track per file, in the folder's colour and
with its output.

![The files organised in their folders](/guide/desktop-en/template-drop-result.png)

If a file is left out, add its word to the rules of the folder it belongs in
and save the template again.

## Create a song with the template

Press **+ New song** in the [Compact View](/docs/compact-view/) or the
[Setlist View](/docs/setlist-view/): it comes with the template's layout. The
arrow next to it (**New song from template**) lets you pick another template,
or **No template**, for that song only.

![New song from template](/guide/desktop-en/new-song-template-menu.png)

1. The arrow next to **+ New song**.
2. The available templates.

Then drop the stems into the song. A notice counts how many were organised and
how many had no rule; **Undo** (or `Ctrl+Z`) takes the whole drop back. Audio
that matches nothing is placed as usual.

## Imported songs

When you import a `.ltpkg` package (from disk, from the cloud or from the
Setlist) or a Reaper or Ableton project, its tracks are organised by the
template just as if you dropped its files: each one goes to its folder by its
name, with its clips and its mix. Tracks that match nothing stay as they come.

## When the template applies

| What you do | Does the template apply? |
| --- | --- |
| **+ New song** | Yes: the song comes with its tracks and folders. |
| Drop files into a song (timeline or Compact) | Yes: each file goes to its place by its name. |
| Drag a folder from the Library | Yes, once per song. |
| Import a `.ltpkg` (disk, cloud or Setlist) | Yes: its tracks are organised by it. |
| Import a one-song Reaper or Ableton project | Yes, and it follows **Each song's tracks**. |
| Import a Reaper or Ableton project with several songs | No: its tracks span every song and stay shared. |
| Split a song in two | It is not applied again. In the per-song modes, the second half gets a copy of the first half's tracks. |
| Open a session you already had | No: its songs stay as they were. |
| **New song from template › No template** | No, for that song only. |

With tracks **shared between songs**, if the session already has tracks with
rules (because you created it from a template), the template does not add
another copy: it only adds its rules to the tracks with the same name.

## Matching rules

Right-click a track or folder › **Matching rules…**: the words, separated by
commas, that send an audio file there.

- Whole words are compared, ignoring accents and case: `ag` does not match
  `stage`. The name the batch shares and the song name don't count.
- When two rules match, the longer one wins (`electric guitar` over `guitar`)
  and, on a tie, the track that comes first.
- On a **track**, the first stem goes to it and the next one makes a sibling
  track. On a **folder**, each stem makes a track inside it.

To keep your changes, **File › Save as template…**.

## Each song's tracks

**Settings › General › Each song's tracks** decides what happens with the
second song and the ones after it:

![Each song's tracks](/guide/desktop-en/settings-song-track-layout.png)

- **Shared between songs**: they all use the same tracks; song 2 fills the
  lanes of song 1.
- **One folder per song**: each song has a folder with its name and its tracks
  inside.
- **Separate per song**: each song has its own copy of the tracks at the root.

In both per-song modes, when you split a song in two, the second half gets its
own copy of the tracks, with its clips.
