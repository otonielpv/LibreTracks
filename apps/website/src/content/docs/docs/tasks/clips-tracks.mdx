A **clip** is a piece of audio placed on a track. A **track** is a row of the
timeline with its volume, its pan and its output. Editing clips **never
changes your audio files**: it only changes which part plays and where.

What each button in the track header does is in
[Ruler, tracks and menus](/docs/interface/timeline/#track-header).

## Selecting clips

- **Click** a clip: selects it (and moves the cursor to that point).
- <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+**click**: adds that clip to the selection or takes it out.
- <kbd>Shift</kbd>+**click**: selects a **rectangle**, from the last clip you
  touched to this one. Every clip on the tracks in between, in that stretch of
  time, is included. It is what you need after splitting several tracks at the
  same point: one gesture and you have all the pieces.
- <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>A</kbd>: every clip.
- <kbd>Esc</kbd>: clears the selection.

A click on an empty part of a track moves the cursor there; dragging across an
empty part scrolls the view.

## Moving clips

Drag the clip. With several selected, they all move together.

- **In time**, the clip snaps to the grid if
  [snap](/docs/interface/toolbar/#view-and-editing) is on.
- Hold <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> while you drag so its edges **snap** to the playback
  cursor, the markers, the edges of songs and the edges of other clips. That
  way a clip lines up exactly with another.
- **To another track**: drag it up or down. While you drag you see which track
  it will land on. The clip **does not jump over a folder**: it stays on the
  last valid track before it. To take it to the other side, open the folder or
  move it in two steps.
- The <kbd>←</kbd> <kbd>→</kbd> arrows move the selection by one grid
  division.

A clip always lives inside a song. If moving it takes it past the end of its
song, the song is lengthened to hold it; if that would make it collide with
the next one, the move doesn't happen and the app tells you why.

## Splitting, duplicating, copying and pasting

**Split** (<kbd>S</kbd>): splits the selected clips at the cursor position.
Also from the clip's menu, **Split At Cursor**.

<Clip
  src="/guide/desktop-en/clip-split.mp4"
  poster="/guide/desktop-en/clip-split.webp"
  caption="Click the ruler to place the cursor, click the clip and press S to split it."
/>

:::tip[Shortening a clip]
Audio clips have no handles for trimming. To remove the start or the end of
one, split it with <kbd>S</kbd> at the point you want and delete the leftover
piece.
:::

- **Duplicate** (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>D</kbd>): puts a copy of the selection
  right after it, on the same tracks.
- **Copy** and **paste** (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>C</kbd>,
  <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>V</kbd>): the copy is pasted right after whatever you
  have selected at that moment (or after the original, if nothing is
  selected), on the same tracks.
- **Delete** (<kbd>Del</kbd> or <kbd>Backspace</kbd>).
- **Colour**: right-click › **Select colour…**. With several clips selected,
  they are all coloured.

Everything can be undone with <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd>.

## Creating tracks

- **Right-click the gap below the tracks**: **Add Audio Track**, **Add Folder
  Track**, **Add MIDI track**, **Add video track** and **Add automation
  track** (there is only one per session).
- **Right-click a track** › **Insert Track** or **Insert Folder Track**: it is
  created right below.
- **Dropping audio** into the gap below the tracks creates a track for it, and
  dragging a Library folder creates one per audio file.

New tracks take a colour from the palette, a different one each time. If you
would rather colour them yourself, turn off *Colour new tracks automatically*
in Settings › General.

## Ordering tracks

Drag the track's **header** up or down. While you drag, the other tracks move
aside to show you the gap where it will land, and the track is shown in its new
place with its clips. With several tracks selected they all move, in the order
they are in.

## Folders

A folder groups tracks: for example, all the drum mics. It folds away to save
space, and its **M**, **S** and volume affect all its tracks at once.

- **Put a track in a folder**: drag it onto the folder's row, towards the
  middle. If you drop it on the top or bottom edge, it ends up above or below
  the folder, outside it.
- **Without dragging**: right-click the track › **Indent Into Previous
  Folder**. With several tracks selected, right-click › **Move to folder…** and
  choose a folder or **New folder…**: they all go in one go.
- **Take it out**: right-click › **Remove From Folder**.
- **Shared audio output**: the tracks inside set to *Inherited (Folder)* go
  out through the folder's output. You change the whole drum kit's in one
  place.

LibreTracks remembers which folders you left folded.

## More track settings

- **Select several tracks**: <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+click or <kbd>Shift</kbd>+click on
  the headers. Whatever you touch on one (M, S, T, volume, pan, output) applies
  to all of them; volume and pan move by the same proportion, so the mix is
  kept.
- **Rename**: select the track and press <kbd>F2</kbd>.
- **Colour**: right-click › **Select colour…**.
- **Height**: drag the bottom edge of the header; with <kbd>Alt</kbd>, you
  change all of them at once, and a double-click goes back to the normal
  height. Also <kbd>Alt</kbd>+mouse wheel over the track (with
  <kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+wheel, all of them).
- **Sum to mono**: mixes the two channels of a stereo track into one. Handy
  for a track that goes out of a mono output on your interface. A *mono* tag
  appears next to the name; **Back to stereo** undoes it.
- **Delete**: right-click › **Delete**, or <kbd>Del</kbd> with the track
  selected. If it has clips, the app asks whether you want to delete it along
  with them.
