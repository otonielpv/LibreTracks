LibreTracks can play **video in sync with the music** on a second screen: the
venue's projector, a TV or a monitor for the band. The video sits on its own
track on the timeline, like the audio, and follows the song when you play,
jump to a marker, Vamp or change the tempo with warp.

## Adding a video

**Drag the video file** from your file manager:

- **onto the timeline**: it is placed where you drop it, on a video track (if
  there is none, one is created);
- **into the Library**: it goes into the **Videos** section. Double-click it,
  or press `+`, to put it on the timeline at the cursor.

![The Videos section of the Library](/guide/desktop-en/library-videos.png)

Formats: **MP4, MOV, MKV, WebM, AVI and MPG**. Like audio, the video is used
**from wherever it is** on your disk, without copying it.

:::tip[So jumps respond straight away]
A video exported with few keyframes takes a while to respond when you jump
somewhere else. If LibreTracks detects it, it flags it with an icon in the
Library. When you export it from your editor, use **one keyframe per
second**.
:::

## The sound of the video

If the video you place **has sound**, LibreTracks asks whether you want to
split it off to a normal audio track:

![The question when placing a video with sound](/guide/desktop-en/video-audio-prompt.png)

- **Extract as audio track**: the sound is saved as one more audio file (a
  WAV in the Library) and placed on a **new audio track below the video**,
  aligned with it. From then on it is a track like any other: it has its
  volume, mute and solo, you send it to whichever output you like and you can
  move or trim it.
- **No**: the video is placed without sound.

:::caution[A video never plays its own sound]
LibreTracks plays **only the picture** of a video. If you do not extract its
audio, that sound is not heard anywhere. That way the same sound never plays
twice, and the video's sound goes through your mix and outputs like
everything else.
:::

If you place several videos at once, the question comes once for all of them.
**Remember my choice** applies the same answer from then on, without asking
again. To change it, or to be asked again, go to **Settings → Video → When
placing a video with sound**: **Ask**, **Always extract the audio to a track**
or **Don't extract the audio**.

Said no to a video and now you want its sound? Right-click the clip →
**Extract audio**.

Since the extracted audio is an ordinary audio file, it **travels in
packages** even if you export without the videos: the song sounds the same on
another computer.

You can also create an empty video track by right-clicking the empty part of
the track list → **Add video track**.

## The video track and clip

![A video track with its clip](/guide/desktop-en/video-track.png)

<Legend>

1. Track **name** (right-click to rename it, change its colour or delete it).
2. **Eye**: shows or hides this track on the output.
3. **S** (solo): shows only this video track.
4. The **clip**, with thumbnails of the video. Drag it to move it.
5. Clip **edges**: drag them to trim the start or the end.
6. **Fade handles**, on the top corners (they appear when you hover): drag
   them to fade the video in or out from black.

</Legend>

With **several video tracks** whose clips overlap in time, the one on the
**top track** is shown. That way you can put a long background below and
short clips above it.

Right-click a clip:

![A video clip's menu](/guide/desktop-en/menu-video-clip.png)

- **Split at cursor** (`S`) and **Duplicate** (`Ctrl+D`).
- **Fit** for this clip: the output's, **Fit (black bars)**, **Fill (crops)**
  to fill the screen, or **Stretch**.
- **Remove fades**, **Color**, **Extract audio** (splits its sound off to an
  audio track, see [above](#the-sound-of-the-video)) and **Delete** (`Del`).

## Choosing the display: the wizard

When you open a session with video and have not chosen a display yet (or the
one you chose is not connected), LibreTracks says so:

![The session-with-video notice](/guide/desktop-en/video-notice.png)

**Set up** opens the wizard (it is also in **Settings → Video → Wizard…**).
Nothing is saved until you press **Done** at the end.

1. **Welcome**.

   ![Wizard, step 1](/guide/desktop-en/video-wizard-1.png)

2. **Choose the display**. Each connected screen shows a big number: pick the
   card with the number you see on the projector. The one marked *where
   LibreTracks is* is the computer's own screen. If you only have one, you can
   **use a window for now**.

   ![Wizard, choosing the display](/guide/desktop-en/video-wizard-2.png)

3. **Check the picture**: a test pattern is projected. If you can see it,
   **Yes, I see it**; if not, **No, try another**.

   ![Wizard, checking the picture](/guide/desktop-en/video-wizard-3.png)

4. **Fit and idle screen**: how the video adapts to the screen (**Fit** with
   black bars, **Fill** cropping, or **Stretch**) and what shows when there is
   no video (**Black** or an **image** of your own, like your church's or
   band's logo).

   ![Wizard, fit and idle screen](/guide/desktop-en/video-wizard-4.png)

5. **Sync** (optional): calibrate the projector's delay now or later (see
   below).

   ![Wizard, sync](/guide/desktop-en/video-wizard-5.png)

6. **Done**: sums up where the video will go.

   ![Wizard, done](/guide/desktop-en/video-wizard-6.png)

## Video settings

Everything in the wizard, and a bit more, is in **Settings → Video**:

![Video settings (1)](/guide/desktop-en/settings-video-1.png)

- **Video output**: turns projection on or off.
- **Display**, with **Refresh** (if you just connected one) and **Identify
  displays** (shows the number on each one).
- **Mode**: **Fullscreen** or **Window**. Double-clicking the video switches
  between them; the window can be moved and resized.
- **Fullscreen always in front**: nothing covers the projector, not even
  notifications. Untick it if you need to put another window on top.

![Video settings (2)](/guide/desktop-en/settings-video-2.png)

- **Fit** and **Idle screen**, as in the wizard.
- **When stopped**: what stays on screen when you stop: the **last frame**,
  the **idle screen** or **black**.
- **Hardware decoding**: **Automatic** uses the graphics card. Turn it off
  only if a video looks wrong.

![Video settings (3)](/guide/desktop-en/settings-video-3.png)

- **Delay compensation** (see below).
- **Test pattern**: projects a test image to focus and frame the projector.
- **When placing a video with sound**: ask, always extract its audio to a
  track, or never extract it (see [The sound of the video](#the-sound-of-the-video)).
- At the bottom, a few sync and frame **statistics**, useful if something goes
  wrong.

### Calibrating the delay

Projectors and many TVs show the picture **slightly late** compared with the
sound. **Delay compensation** fixes it: a positive value brings the picture
forward.

With **Calibrate…**, turn the metronome on and press Play: the output shows a
**white flash on every beat**. Move the slider until flash and click line up.
Or measure it: tap 10 times when you **see** the flash and another 10 when you
**hear** the click, and LibreTracks suggests the value. Do it **at the venue**,
with the real projector and sound.

## Live

- **Emergency black**: the **`B`** key blacks out the projection instantly,
  and another press brings it back. There are also actions for **fade to
  black**, **show the idle screen** and **turn the output on or off**, which you
  can assign keys to in [Settings → Shortcuts](/docs/interface/settings/) or a
  MIDI control.
- The **Remote** has a **Black** button to do it from the tablet. See
  [Custom Remote](/docs/remote-control/).
- In the top bar, an **indicator** shows which display the video goes out on
  and warns if it is disconnected ("Video: display disconnected") or black. If
  the projector drops out mid-show, the music keeps playing and the indicator
  tells you.

## Taking the videos to another computer

When you [export](/docs/integration-ecosystem/) a song or a session that has
video, an **Include the videos in the package** checkbox appears, with how
much they take up. Without them the package is much lighter, but you will
have to copy the videos separately and locate them again when you open it.
When you import a package with videos, LibreTracks asks whether you want to
**import the videos too** and checks they fit.

On **Android and iPhone/iPad** the video also goes out by cable (USB‑C to
HDMI, or the Lightning Digital AV adapter). **Settings → Video** on the phone
has a guide to connecting it. If you lock the phone, the projector turns off
until you unlock it.
