LibreTracks speaks MIDI both ways:

- **Input**: a pedal, a pad or a controller drives the app (play, jump,
  Vamp…).
- **Output**: a **MIDI track** sends notes, controls and program changes at
  exact moments in the song, to a keyboard, the lighting desk or the lyrics
  software.

## Connecting a controller

In **Settings › MIDI**, choose the **MIDI input device**. If you plug it in
with the app open, press the refresh button next to it.

![The MIDI tab in Settings](/guide/desktop-en/settings-midi.png)

If the cable comes loose mid-show, the **MIDI controller disconnected** notice
appears in the top bar, and the app opens it again by itself as soon as you
plug it back in. There is no need to go to Settings.

On mobile, **Bluetooth** controllers work too: in the MIDI tab, **Search
Bluetooth devices**.

## Assigning pedals and buttons (MIDI Learn)

How to assign them is in
[Settings › MIDI Learn](/docs/interface/settings/#midi-learn). What you can
control:

- **Transport**: play, pause, stop, previous and next song.
- **Jumps**: to each section marker and each song (the *Markers* and *Songs*
  tabs), cancel the jump, and the mode, bars and transition of jumps.
- **Vamp**: on and off, mode (section or bars) and number of bars.
- **Pitch**: raise, lower or reset the transposition of the selected song;
  select the previous or next song.
- **Switches**: metronome and voice guide.
- **Values with a rotary control** (CC): BPM, metronome volume, bars for jumps
  and for Vamp.
- **Video**: black, fade to black, idle screen and turning the output on.
- **Session**: new, open, save, save as, undo, redo and create a marker at the
  cursor.

## Sending MIDI from the song

### 1. Choose the output

In **Settings › MIDI**, **MIDI output** is the default port MIDI tracks send
through. **Send test note** checks that it gets to the other side.

### 2. Create a MIDI track

Right-click the gap below the tracks, or a track, › **Add MIDI track**. You can
have several: one for each device you want to control.

Its menu (right-click the header):

![A MIDI track's menu](/guide/desktop-en/menu-midi-track.png)

- **MIDI routing**: the **port** (the cable) and the **channel** (1 to 16) its
  messages go out on. *Default output* uses the port from Settings.

![A track's MIDI routing](/guide/desktop-en/midi-route.png)

- **Disable MIDI track**: stops sending without deleting anything (also with
  the power button on the header). Handy in a rehearsal without lights.

### 3. Add messages

Right-click the MIDI track, at the exact point › **Add MIDI here**. The editor
opens:

![The MIDI clip editor](/guide/desktop-en/midi-clip-editor.png)

- **Name**: to recognise it on the timeline (*Chorus lights*).
- **Add message**, of four kinds:
  - **Note**: note, velocity and duration (0 = a single hit).
  - **Control**: a CC with its value.
  - **Program**: a program change (switching sound or scene).
  - **Control curve**: a CC that goes from one value to another over a period
    of time, for smooth fades.
- **Offset (s)**: how long after the clip's point that message goes out.
- **Channel (optional)**: so a message goes out on a different channel from
  the track's.
- Messages that coincide in time go out in the order of the list; use **Move
  up** and **Move down** to change it.
- **Test MIDI** sends the messages right now, without saving, to check that
  the device responds.

The clip shows on the track as a marker with its name. Right-click it:

![A MIDI clip's menu](/guide/desktop-en/menu-midi-clip.png)

**Edit MIDI** or **Delete MIDI**. MIDI clips can be dragged to move them, they
travel with their song when you move it and they go inside exported songs.

## LibreTracks as a MIDI device (mobile)

On Android and iOS, LibreTracks can publish itself as a **virtual MIDI
device**: other apps on the same phone or tablet see it as an input and output
port. Turn it on in Settings › MIDI › **Publish LibreTracks virtual MIDI
port**.
