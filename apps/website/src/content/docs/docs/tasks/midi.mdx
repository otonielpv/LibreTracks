LibreTracks speaks MIDI both ways:

- **Input**: a pedal, a pad or a controller drives the app (play, jump,
  Vamp…).
- **Output**: a **MIDI track** sends notes, controls and program changes at
  exact moments in the song, to a keyboard, the lighting desk or the lyrics
  software.

## Connecting a controller

In **Settings › MIDI**, choose the **MIDI input device**. If you plug it in
with the app open, press the refresh button next to it.

![The MIDI tab in Settings](/guide/desktop-en/settings-midi-1.png)

If the cable comes loose mid-show, the **MIDI controller disconnected** notice
appears in the top bar, and the app opens it again by itself as soon as you
plug it back in. There is no need to go to Settings.

On mobile, **Bluetooth** controllers work too: in the MIDI tab, **Search
Bluetooth devices**.

## Assigning pedals and buttons (MIDI Learn)

How to assign them is in
[Settings › MIDI Learn](/docs/interface/settings/#midi-learn). What you can
control:

- **Transport**: play, pause, stop, previous and next song.
- **Jumps**: to each section marker and each song (the *Markers* and *Songs*
  tabs), cancel the jump, and the mode, bars and transition of jumps.
- **Vamp**: on and off, mode (section or bars) and number of bars.
- **Pitch**: raise, lower or reset the transposition of the selected song;
  select the previous or next song.
- **Switches**: metronome and voice guide.
- **Values with a rotary control** (CC): BPM, metronome volume, bars for jumps
  and for Vamp.
- **Video**: black, fade to black, idle screen and turning the output on.
- **Session**: new, open, save, save as, undo, redo and create a marker at the
  cursor.

## Fader controllers (nanoKONTROL2, Mackie…)

Besides pedals and buttons, LibreTracks works with a fader controller, like
Ableton.

### MIDI Map mode

Press `Ctrl+M` (or **MIDI Learn** in **Settings › MIDI Learn**). Everything
you can assign is tinted: each track's fader, pan, mute and solo in the DAW,
the Compact View and the Setlist View, the song master, the click, the guide,
the pads, the scene buttons and the jumps in Live and Setlist. Controls that
already have a binding show in green, with their message on top.

With a track's **equalizer** open, each band's frequency, gain, Q and on
switch are tinted too, and **Bypass**: an encoder or fader moves frequency and
Q on a log scale (half way is ~630 Hz) and gain in dB (half way is 0 dB). EQ
controls always drive that track, not the bank strips.

![MIDI Map mode](/guide/desktop-en/midi-map-mode.png)

1. The mode's banner. **Exit** (or `Esc`) closes it; the arrow moves it up or
   down if it covers something.
2. A mappable control. Tap it and move the physical control: it is bound.

### Strips 1-8, profiles and motorized faders

In **Settings › MIDI Learn**:

![MIDI Learn: profiles and mixer](/guide/desktop-en/midi-learn-mixer.png)

1. **MIDI Learn** turns MIDI Map mode on.
2. **Controller profile**: if LibreTracks recognises your controller by its
   port name, it suggests the profile. **Apply** fills in the strips, the
   transport and the banks at once. There are profiles for the **Korg
   nanoKONTROL2**, **Mackie Control** and the **M-Vave SMC-Mixer** (in its
   Mackie mode). **Import…** and **Export** load or save your own bindings.
3. The list's views:
   - **Mixer**: **strips 1 to 8** (volume, pan, mute and solo), which follow
     the order of the visible tracks of the playing song, plus **Next bank**
     and **Previous bank** to move 8 at a time. The 8 tracks you control get
     a red edge on their header.
   - **Scenes**: see [Mix scenes](/docs/live-scenes/).
   - **Bindings**: everything assigned, with its **Mode** (absolute,
     relative, toggle or momentary button), **Min**, **Max**,
     **Invert** and **Takeover**.

For the button LEDs and motorized faders to follow the mix, pick the
**Controller output (LEDs)** in **Settings › MIDI**: the port your controller
listens on for state, usually named like its input.

**Takeover mode** (**Settings › MIDI**) decides what happens when the physical
fader isn't where the track is, for example after changing bank or song:

- **None**: the track jumps to wherever the fader is.
- **Pick-up**: the fader does nothing until it reaches the track's value.
- **Value scaling**: the fader moves the track straight away and both meet at
  the end of the travel. It never jumps or goes dead.

## Sending MIDI from the song

### 1. Choose the output

In **Settings › MIDI**, **MIDI output** is the default port MIDI tracks send
through. **Send test note** checks that it gets to the other side.

### 2. Create a MIDI track

Right-click the gap below the tracks, or a track, › **Add MIDI track**. On
mobile, tap a track and, in the bottom panel, the three dots (**More actions**)
› **Add MIDI track**. You can have several: one for each device you want to
control. The device does not need to be connected: the messages are prepared
in the song and go out through whatever port you choose later.

Its menu (right-click the header):

![A MIDI track's menu](/guide/desktop-en/menu-midi-track.png)

- **MIDI routing**: the **port** (the cable) and the **channel** (1 to 16) its
  messages go out on. *Default output* uses the port from Settings.

![A track's MIDI routing](/guide/desktop-en/midi-route.png)

- **Disable MIDI track**: stops sending without deleting anything (also with
  the power button on the header). Handy in a rehearsal without lights.

### 3. Add messages

Right-click the MIDI track, at the exact point › **Add MIDI here**. On mobile,
tap the MIDI track and press **Add MIDI here** in the bottom panel: it goes
where the playhead is. The editor opens:

![The MIDI clip editor](/guide/desktop-en/midi-clip-editor.png)

- **Name**: to recognise it on the timeline (*Chorus lights*).
- **Add message**, of four kinds:
  - **Note**: note, velocity and duration (0 = a single hit).
  - **Control**: a CC with its value.
  - **Program**: a program change (switching sound or scene).
  - **Control curve**: a CC that goes from one value to another over a period
    of time, for smooth fades.
- **Offset (s)**: how long after the clip's point that message goes out.
- **Channel (optional)**: so a message goes out on a different channel from
  the track's.
- Messages that coincide in time go out in the order of the list; use **Move
  up** and **Move down** to change it.
- **Test MIDI** sends the messages right now, without saving, to check that
  the device responds.
- **Lead time**: so the clip goes out before its section starts. See the next
  step.
- **Collapse all** and **Expand all** fold or open every message at once. The
  editor remembers which ones you left folded.

The clip shows on the track as a marker with its name. Right-click it:

![A MIDI clip's menu](/guide/desktop-en/menu-midi-clip.png)

**Edit MIDI**, **Duplicate** or **Delete MIDI**. MIDI clips can be dragged to
move them, they travel with their song when you move it and they go inside
exported songs.

### 4. Sending it ahead of the section (lead time)

The typical case is lyrics software (ProPresenter, for example): the command
that changes the lyrics has to arrive **a little before** the section starts,
so the audience already has the words on screen when the singing begins.

Place the clip **right on the section marker** and, in the editor, choose the
**Lead time**:

- The shortcuts: **Half a beat**, **1 beat**, **2 beats**, **1 bar** or
  **2 bars**.
- **Custom...**: any amount, with decimals, in **beats**, **bars** or
  **seconds**.

A beat is one metronome click: it follows the tempo, its changes and ramps,
the time signature (in 6/8 a beat is an eighth note) and warp. If you change
the song's tempo, "1 beat" is still one beat. Use **seconds** if you want it
fixed.

The track shows a dashed line from the point where it really goes out to the
marker.

What matters is that the lead is measured against **what is really going to
play**, not against what comes next on the timeline:

- **Normal playback and arrangements**: it goes out the lead before its
  marker.
- **A live jump**: if you are in the verse and arm a jump at the end of the
  section to verse 2 (instead of going on to the chorus), the **verse 2** clip
  goes out, the lead before the jump. The chorus clip does not, because the
  chorus is not going to play.
- **If you arm the jump late**, once the lead point has passed, the target's
  clip goes out right then.
- **If you cancel the jump** after the chorus clip should have gone out, the
  chorus clip goes out at that moment.
- **Vamp**: the clip at the start of the loop goes out again before every
  lap.
- **If you jump or press Play inside the lead** (half a beat before verse 2,
  for example), it goes out at once: it is what should be on screen.

To see what is sent and when, use a MIDI monitor (Pocket MIDI, or MIDI-OX on
Windows) listening to the same port.

### Selecting, duplicating and copying

- **Ctrl+click** (**Cmd+click** on Mac) selects a clip without opening the
  editor. **Shift+click** adds every clip on that track between the last one
  picked and this one.
- <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>D</kbd> duplicates the selection right after it: the
  group's length, rounded up to whole bars. The copy keeps its messages, colour
  and lead time, and ends up selected, so you can chain copies.
- <kbd>Del</kbd> deletes the selection.
- With several selected, right-clicking one of them offers **Duplicate** and
  **Delete** for all of them.
- Hold <kbd>Alt</kbd> (<kbd>Option</kbd> on Mac) **when you drop** a drag and
  a copy lands where you drop it; the original stays put. Onto another MIDI
  track too.

## LibreTracks as a MIDI device (mobile)

On Android and iOS, LibreTracks can publish itself as a **virtual MIDI
device**: other apps on the same phone or tablet see it as an input and output
port. Turn it on in Settings › MIDI › **Publish LibreTracks virtual MIDI
port**.
