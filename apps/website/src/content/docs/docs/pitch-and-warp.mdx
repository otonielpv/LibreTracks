Three controls decide how each song sounds, and it pays not to mix them up:

| Control | Where it is | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Transpose** | Toolbar, with the song selected | Raises or lowers the pitch, from −12 to +12 semitones |
| **Warp** | Toolbar, with the song selected | Separates pitch and speed: the audio follows the timeline's tempo without going out of tune |
| **T** | Each track's header | Decides whether that track changes pitch when you transpose (only with warp) |

The model is the same as Warp in Ableton Live.

## Transposing a song

1. Click the song's name on the ruler to select it.
2. In the toolbar, open **Transpose** with its settings button.
3. Press <kbd>+</kbd> or <kbd>−</kbd>: each press is one semitone.

  poster="/guide/desktop-en/transpose-varispeed.webp"
  caption="+2 semitones with warp off: the song goes up in pitch and also gets shorter, and its key goes from D to E."
/>

What happens depends on warp:

- **Warp off** (the usual starting point): it works like speeding up or slowing
  down a tape. Raising the pitch **shortens** the song and lowering it
  lengthens it. The following songs move by themselves so they don't overlap.
  Every track changes, without exception.
- **Warp on**: the pitch changes and the length **doesn't**. It is what you
  want almost always live.

The song's key (the letter next to its name) is transposed by itself, and the
*+2 st* label reminds you it is transposed.

## Turning warp on

With the song selected, open **Warp** in the toolbar and flip the switch.

<Clip
  src="/guide/desktop-en/warp-on.mp4"
  poster="/guide/desktop-en/warp-on.webp"
  caption="When warp is turned on, the song keeps its new pitch and gets its original length back."
/>

When you turn it on, LibreTracks saves the song's current tempo as its
**original BPM**: the tempo the audio was recorded at. From then on:

- If you change the song's **BPM**, the audio stretches or shrinks to follow
  it, **without changing pitch**. The panel shows it as *140 → 132 (×0.943)*.
- If the original BPM isn't right (for example, the audio was recorded at 72
  and the session said 70), fix it in the song's menu › **Change Original
  BPM**.

:::caution[Warp uses CPU]
Each track with warp costs more CPU than one without. On a modest computer or
phone, turn it on only for the songs that need it and keep an eye on the
[resource meter](/docs/interface/main-screen/#readouts-and-resource-meter).
:::

## The T button: tracks that shouldn't change pitch

When you transpose, the **click**, the **spoken guide** or a **pad** recorded
in its own key shouldn't change. That is what each track's **T** button is
for:

- **T on** (the default): the track follows the transposition.
- **T off**: the track stays in its original pitch, but follows warp's tempo,
  so it doesn't drift out of sync.

The **T** button only has an effect with **warp on**. With warp off,
transposing changes the speed, and a track that didn't change it would drift
out of sync instantly; that is why, without warp, every track changes.

| Warp | Transpose | Track's T | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Off | 0 | any | Original audio |
| Off | ≠ 0 | any | Changes pitch **and** length (every track) |
| On | 0 | any | Follows the tempo; original pitch |
| On | ≠ 0 | on | Changes pitch; length set by warp |
| On | ≠ 0 | off | Original pitch; length set by warp |

## A recipe for live

1. Build the session with the BPM each song was recorded at and warp off.
2. If the band asks for the song **faster or slower**, turn warp on and change
   the BPM.
3. If they ask for **another key**, turn warp on and transpose. Turn off the
   **T** on the click and guide tracks.
4. Make pitch changes **before pressing Play**, or between songs. Changing
   pitch while it plays makes the engine rearrange its voices and, on modest
   computers, can cause small dropouts.

## Why markers don't move from their musical place

Markers, song edges and tempo and time-signature changes are stored at the
position of the **original audio**. When warp or transposition changes a
song's length, everything moves with it and keeps landing on the same musical
point: the Chorus still starts where the Chorus starts.
