Before getting into the buttons, it helps to know the pieces LibreTracks works
with. There are only a few, and almost everything else follows from them.

## The session: your setlist

A **session** is what you take to the show: one or more songs in order, with
their tracks, their mix and their outputs. It is what you open, save and take
to another computer.

On disk, a session is **a folder** with:

- the **`.ltsession`** file, the one you open;
- an **`audio`** folder, where audio that arrives **inside a package** ends up
  when you import it (an `.ltpkg` or a full `.ltset`);
- a **`cache`** folder with the waveforms (they are rebuilt if you delete it).

Your audio files are **not copied** into the session: LibreTracks plays them
from wherever they are. So if you move or delete an original, that track goes
silent (LibreTracks tells you and lets you locate it again). To take a session
to another computer, export it; see
[Export, import and share](/docs/integration-ecosystem/).

## The song: the main piece

Inside a session everything is organised in **songs**. On the timeline each
song is a named stretch of the ruler (the **song bar**), and everything below
it belongs to it: its clips, its markers, its tempo.

What matters most:

- **Every clip belongs to one song.** Move a song and everything in it moves;
  delete it and everything in it goes.
- **Many settings belong to the song, not the session**: the **tempo** and
  **time signature**, the **key**, the **transposition** and **warp**, and its
  **master volume**. That way each song in the setlist can be in its own key
  and at its own speed.
- Live, you jump **between songs** with a transition (at the end, at the next
  marker, with a fade…).

Songs are created from a stretch of the timeline, from a Library folder, by
dropping audio files or by importing an `.ltpkg`. See [Songs](/docs/tasks/songs/).

## Tracks

**Tracks** are the rows of the timeline. They belong to the whole session: the
"Drums" track is the same in every song, even if each song has different clips
on it (or none).

| Track | What for |
| --- | --- |
| **Audio** | Holds the audio clips: the song's stems, the click, the guide. |
| **Folder** | Groups other tracks. Its volume, mute and solo apply to all of them, and it can set the output for all of them at once. |
| **MIDI** | Sends MIDI messages to other gear (lighting desk, lyrics). See [MIDI](/docs/tasks/midi/). |
| **Video** | Holds the videos that are projected. See [Video](/docs/tasks/video/). |
| **Automation** | A special row with actions at an exact time: jump, mute, move a fader… See [Automation](/docs/automation/). |

Each track has an **output**: where it sends its sound (Master, a pair of
headphones, another output of your interface). See
[Audio outputs and metronome](/docs/audio-routing-metronome/).

Tracks that LibreTracks creates on its own when you drop an audio file are
deleted automatically when they are left without clips. Tracks you create
yourself are never deleted on their own.

## Clips

A **clip** is a piece of an audio file placed on a track, at a point in a
song. It is only a **reference**: trimming, splitting or duplicating it never
changes the original file. See [Clips and tracks](/docs/tasks/clips-tracks/).

## The Library

The **Library** is the list of every audio file (and video) in the session,
whether it is on the timeline or not. From there you drag files to the
timeline, sort them into **folders** (which only exist in the Library: they
do not move the files) and create songs from a folder. See
[Library and audio](/docs/tasks/library/).

## Markers

**Markers** point at moments in the song. There are two kinds:

- **Sections** (Intro, Verse, Chorus…): the destinations for jumping live,
  announced by the [voice guide](/docs/voice-guide/).
- **Cues** (Drums in, Last time…): directions for the band, which also show in
  the Remote and in the Live View.

Dragging a marker from one row to the other changes its kind. See
[Markers](/docs/tasks/markers/).

## Tempo and time signature

Each song has its own **tempo** and **time signature**, and can change them
midway with a tempo or time-signature change on the ruler. The click, the
grid and jumps "after a few bars" follow them. See
[Tempo and time signature](/docs/tasks/tempo/).

## Three ways to see the same thing

The session can be shown three ways, and what you change in one shows in the
others (`Tab` switches between them):

- The **DAW view**, the timeline: for building and editing.
- The [Compact View](/docs/compact-view/): each song in a column, for
  arranging the setlist and mixing.
- The [Live View](/docs/live-view/): big buttons for playing live.

And from another device, the [Remote](/docs/remote-control/).
