Everything you prepare in LibreTracks can travel: a single song into another
musician's session, the whole session to the show computer or to your phone,
or a mix as a WAV for whoever needs it. This page explains each route.

| You want to… | Use | File |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Move **one song** into another session | Export song | `.ltpkg` |
| Take **the whole session** to another computer | Export session | `.ltset` |
| Start new sessions with **your tracks and outputs** | Save as template | `.lttemplate` |
| Send **an audio file** (mix or stems) | Render audio | `.wav` / `.zip` |
| Keep it all **in the cloud** | Import / Export → Google Drive | — |

:::note[Your audio is not copied when you import it]
When you add audio to a session, LibreTracks plays it **from wherever it is**
on your disk; it does not make a copy. So if you are taking the session to
another computer, export it in **Full** mode: that is when the audio goes
inside the file. On a phone it works the same (except for files that live in
the cloud, which are copied).
:::

## Exporting a song (.ltpkg)

Right-click the **song bar** on the ruler and choose **Export Song**.

![The Export song dialog](/guide/desktop-en/export-song-modal.png)

<Legend>

1. **Full (for sharing)**: puts your original audio files inside, untouched.
   The package opens on any computer.
2. **Light**: carries no audio, it only points at your files by their path.
   It is tiny, but it only works on this same computer.

</Legend>

The package carries the song with its tracks, clips, markers, tempo, key,
transposition and outputs. If the song has videos, a checkbox to include them
appears too.

The file name is suggested from the song's details, for example
`Dios es Real [140bpm D 4-4].ltpkg`, so you can recognise it at a glance in a
folder or in the cloud.

### Importing a song

An `.ltpkg` song is **added** to the session you have open, at the end of the
setlist. You can:

- Use **File → Import song**.
- Press **Import .ltpkg** in the [Compact View](/docs/compact-view/).
- **Drag** the `.ltpkg` from your file manager onto the timeline or the
  Compact View.

If a track in the package has the same name as one you already have, its
clips go to that track instead of creating another one.

## Exporting the whole session (.ltset)

An `.ltset` carries **the whole session** in a single file: every song, the
library, the automation and the waveforms. It is how you prepare it at home
and open it as it is on the show computer, or move it to your phone.

Open **File → Export session…**

![The Export session dialog](/guide/desktop-en/export-session-modal.png)

<Legend>

1. **Full (to take with you)**: includes your original audio files (an MP3
   stays an MP3). It opens on any computer; when it opens, LibreTracks
   prepares the audio, which takes a while on phones and slow computers.
2. **Optimized (for phones and slow machines)**: instead of the originals it
   carries the audio **already prepared** for playback, so the session opens
   instantly. It is bigger and cannot be used to recover the originals: keep
   them somewhere else.
3. **Light**: no audio, it points at your files by their path. Only for this
   same computer.

</Legend>

Only the audio **your clips use** is packed, not the whole library. While it
exports, an indicator shows the progress and you can keep working.

### Importing a session

Use **File → Import session…**, or the **Import session** button on the start
screen (nothing needs to be open). Pick the `.ltset`, choose **where to save**
the session, and LibreTracks creates its folder there and opens it. Unlike a
song, a session is **not merged** into the one you had: it opens as a new
session.

## The cloud: your Google Drive

LibreTracks can keep songs and sessions in **your own Google Drive
account**, to move them from the computer to your phone or another computer
without cables or USB sticks. LibreTracks has no servers: the files are in
your Drive, in a `LibreTracks` folder, and the app **can only see the files it
created**, nothing else in your account.

With the account connected, **Import** and **Export** (of songs and sessions)
first ask where:

![Choosing between this device and Google Drive](/guide/desktop-en/storage-choice.png)

- **Export → Google Drive** uploads the file to your account. For a song it
  asks after you pick the mode; for a session, before.
- **Import → Google Drive** shows what you have uploaded, with search and
  filters, and downloads it.

The **Cloud** button (in **File → Cloud…** and on the start screen) does not
upload or download anything: it is for **managing the account**.

![The cloud panel](/guide/desktop-en/cloud-panel.png)

- **Connect Google Drive** opens the browser so you can sign in and grant
  access. The first time, that is all the panel shows.
- **Disconnect** removes the account from this device. Nothing in your Drive
  is deleted.
- The **space** used and free is that of **your whole Google account**, shared
  with Gmail and Photos.
- The **Sessions** and **Songs** lists show what you uploaded, with its size,
  and a **Delete** button to remove it from Drive.

The connection is kept on each device: on your phone you connect the account
there too.

## Rendering audio

To send the song to someone who does not use LibreTracks (the musician who has
to learn it, a sound engineer, a recording), render it to WAV: right-click the
song bar and choose **Render audio…**.

![The Render audio dialog](/guide/desktop-en/render-modal.png)

<Legend>

1. **All** / **None**, and how many tracks are ticked.
2. The song's **tracks**. Tick the ones you want; a folder's checkbox ticks or
   unticks all of its tracks.
3. **Mix (one file)**: the ticked tracks summed into a single WAV.
4. **Separate tracks (.zip)**: one WAV per track, all aligned to the start of
   the song, inside a `.zip`.
5. **Format** (16-bit, 24-bit or 32-bit float WAV), **sample rate** (the
   project's by default) and **channels** (stereo or mono).
6. **Apply the mix**: the volume and pan of each track and folder, and the
   song's master, as they sound. Unticked, everything comes out at 0 dB and
   centred.
7. **Normalize**: adjusts each file so its peak sits at −0.3 dB.
8. **Include the metronome**.
9. **Include the voice guide**. With separate tracks, the metronome and the
   guide each come out as their own file.
10. **File name**. It is suggested from what you pick, for example
    "Dios es Real (no Vocals)" if you leave out a track.

</Legend>

The render happens on the side, without interrupting whatever is playing, and
**Stop** cancels it. If an audio file could not be read, it comes out silent
and you are told at the end.

## Session templates

If you always set up the same tracks (click, guide, drums, bass… with their
folders, colours and outputs), save them as a template and start each new
session with everything in place.

- **File → Save as template…** creates a `.lttemplate` file with the
  **structure**: tracks, folders, names, colours and outputs. On purpose it
  does **not** keep clips, songs, markers, tempo or the mix (volumes, mute…),
  so each session starts clean.
- **File → New from template…**, or the **Templates** list on the
  [start screen](/docs/interface/main-screen/), creates a new session from it.
  **Use template file…** opens one you keep in another folder.

Since it is a file, you can hand it to another engineer or take it to another
computer.

## Importing Reaper and Ableton projects

If the song is built in Reaper or in Ableton Live, there is no need to export
the stems and place them again: **File → Import Reaper/Ableton** (or the
button on the start screen) reads the `.rpp` or `.als` project and creates the
tracks and clips.

- With a session open, the project is added **at the end of the setlist**.
- From the start screen, you first pick the project and then where to save
  the new session.
- You can also **drag** an `.rpp` or `.als` onto the timeline (it lands where
  you drop it, or at the end if it would overlap another song) or onto the
  Compact View (it is added as a new song at the end).

What matters for playing live is kept, not every detail of the DAW:

- **Reaper**: audio items become clips; tracks and folders are recreated with
  their basic mix; tempo and time-signature changes are imported; each
  **region** becomes a song and each **marker**, a section marker in it.
- **Ableton Live**: the Arrangement's audio clips become clips on their
  tracks; **locators** become section markers; tempo and time signature are
  imported. The whole Arrangement is **one song**, because in Ableton locators
  mark sections, not songs.

The DAW's plugins and effects are not imported: LibreTracks plays the audio
of the clips.

## LibreTracks files

| File | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `.ltsession` | The session you work in (inside its folder). |
| `.ltpkg` | An exported song. |
| `.ltset` | An exported whole session. |
| `.lttemplate` | A session template. |

On Windows each type has its own icon in File Explorer, and a double-click
opens it in LibreTracks. On macOS and Linux they are also associated with the
app, with its general icon.
