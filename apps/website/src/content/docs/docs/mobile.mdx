LibreTracks on a phone or tablet is **the same app** as on the computer: the
same audio engine, the same session format and the same DAW, Compact and Live
views. A session built on the computer opens as it is on the phone, and the
other way round.

It installs from **Google Play** on Android and from the **App Store** on
iPhone and iPad (see [System requirements](/docs/system-requirements/)). On a
phone the app always runs **in landscape**.

## Start screen

When you open the app you see **Your sessions**, the list of sessions saved
on the device. From there you create a new session, open an existing one or
import an `.ltset`. The bin icon deletes a session from the device, with its
audio and its cache; the session you have open cannot be deleted.

The **Demo song** opens a sample session that comes with the app, with two
songs and six tracks, to try the transport, the mix and the jumps without
importing anything. It always opens the same session: pressing it several
times does not create copies.

## Importing audio without copying it

As on the computer, when you import audio the session **uses your original
files** instead of duplicating them. That way a multitrack takes up on the
phone what its files take up and nothing more.

![The Import without copying audio into the session setting](/guide/mobile-en/settings-import-no-copy.png)

The setting is **Settings → General → Import without copying audio into the
session**, and it is on by default.

- If you move or delete an original file, its track goes silent and the file
  shows up in **Missing files**, where you can link it again.
- Files that live **in the cloud** (Google Drive, or "Recent" in the file
  picker, for example) cannot be read directly: those are always copied.
- **Turn it off** if you would rather have each audio file copied into the
  session; for example, if you will delete the originals from the phone after
  importing. It only affects what you import from then on.
- Deleting a track or a session **never** deletes your original files: only
  what is inside the session's folder is deleted.

When you import several files, each one shows up in the library as soon as it
starts being prepared, with its progress, without waiting for the whole batch.

## Your folders in the Library

The Library on the phone also has **Your folders**: with **Add folder** you
pick a folder on the phone (or on the SD card) and browse it from the app,
without importing file by file through the picker.

- **Tap** audio files to select them and press **Add to timeline**: each one
  goes to a new track at the cursor position.
- The **+** button on each folder adds it whole **as a song** at the cursor,
  with its name and one track per audio file.
- The folder stays saved even if you close the app or restart the phone. When
  you remove it from the Library, LibreTracks gives back the access permission
  you granted.
- **In this session** works like an accordion: when you expand it, the
  session's audio takes the whole Library and your folders step aside; when
  you collapse it, they come back.

## Where sessions are kept (Android)

If your Android has a **microSD card**, or you plug in a **USB stick or drive
(OTG)**, you can keep sessions there instead of in internal storage. It is set
in **Settings → General → Where to keep sessions**. If the phone only has
internal storage, the setting does not appear.

- The list shows each volume with the name Android gives it (for example
  "SanDisk SD card" or "USB drive") and the free space it has left.
- **It only affects new sessions.** The ones you already have keep opening
  from where they are, and they all show together in "Your sessions".
- **The audio cache goes with the sessions.** It is what takes the most room,
  so when you pick the card, the cache is kept there too and does not fill the
  internal storage.
- If you **remove the card or the USB drive**, LibreTracks notices straight
  away: it warns in Settings and keeps new sessions in internal storage until
  you connect it again. When it is mounted again, everything updates on its
  own, without restarting the app.

LibreTracks keeps sessions in its own folder on each volume, so it does not
ask for any storage permission.

:::caution[USB drives and cards]
Wait for a save to finish before you unplug a USB drive. If it is removed
halfway, the session stays as it was at the last complete save and is not
lost, but changes that were not saved yet are.
:::

## Gestures on the timeline

On the computer you use the mouse and right-click. On a phone it works like
this:

| Gesture | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **One finger** | Scrolls in any direction: left and right move through time, up and down go through the tracks. |
| **Two-finger pinch** | Zooms only. |
| **Tap** | Selects what is underneath: a clip, a marker, a track or a song. Tapping an empty spot clears the selection. |
| **Drag something already selected** | Moves it. You tap it first and then drag it, so an accidental swipe does not move the audio. |
| **Press and hold** | Opens the menu at the exact spot where you put your finger. |

There are no navigate and edit modes to switch between: it all depends on
whether what you drag was selected.

### The action bar

What right-click shows on the computer shows on a phone in a **floating bar**
above the timeline. When you tap something, the bar shows what you can do with
it, and with nothing selected it offers to **add** a section, a cue, audio or
video. If there are more actions than fit, the dots button opens the full
list. The arrow tucks the bar away to see more timeline.

With **several tracks selected**, the bar includes a mix button that changes
the volume, pan, output and transposition of all of them at once. Volume and
pan move relative to each other and keep the mix they already had.

### Tracks and folders

Dragging a track on a phone **only changes its order**. To put tracks in a
folder, select them and choose **Move to folder…** in their menu, to an
existing folder or a new one. To take them out, **Remove From Folder**. That
way you do not have to hit the middle of the folder's row with your finger.

## The back button (Android)

Android's back button **closes whatever you have open first** (a setting, a
menu, a panel) instead of leaving the app. If nothing is open and something is
playing, the first press warns ("Playing. Press back again to exit.") and you
need a second press to leave, so you do not cut off a rehearsal by accident.

## Audio output

In **Settings → Audio** you choose the output. If you connect a **USB audio
interface** (with an OTG cable on Android, or with the camera adapter or USB‑C
on iPhone and iPad), it shows up there with its channels and is routed as on
the computer (see [Audio outputs and metronome](/docs/audio-routing-metronome/)).
A phone has a single audio system, so there is no ASIO or WASAPI selector.

On **Android** there is also a **Low latency** setting:

![The Low latency setting on Android](/guide/mobile-en/settings-low-latency.png)

- **Off** (the default), LibreTracks uses Android's normal playback output.
  That is why you hear the system's sound settings, like Dolby Atmos, the
  equaliser or the surround effect, as in any music player. If you do not want
  them, turn them off in Android's sound settings.
- **On**, it uses Android's fast output: less delay, especially with USB
  interfaces, but without the system effects and, on modest phones, with a
  risk of dropouts. Turn it on if you play live over the tracks and notice the
  delay.

If the app goes unused for a good while, the phone can put the audio to sleep
to save battery; when you come back, **Resume** wakes it up (see
[Troubleshooting](/docs/help/troubleshooting/#the-app-was-idle-appears)).

## MIDI and video

- **MIDI**: connect a controller or a MIDI interface over USB (OTG on Android,
  an adapter on iPhone and iPad). On iPhone and iPad you can also send and
  receive MIDI **over Wi‑Fi** with a Mac (Audio MIDI Setup → Network). See
  [MIDI](/docs/tasks/midi/).
- **Video**: videos are added from the gallery or from files with the **Add**
  button in the action bar, and go out to a projector or a TV connected by
  cable: USB‑C to HDMI (the phone needs video out over USB‑C) or the Lightning
  Digital AV adapter; on iPhone and iPad also over AirPlay, experimental for
  now (calibrate the delay at the venue). **Settings → Video** has a guide to
  connecting it. Locking the phone turns the projector off. See
  [Video on the projector](/docs/tasks/video/).

## Audio cache

Audio that is not WAV (MP3, FLAC…) is prepared once and kept in the cache, so
it loads instantly next time. On a phone the cache folder cannot be changed:
it always goes with the volume where you keep sessions. In **Settings** you
can see how big it is, set a limit and clear it.

## Moving sessions from the computer to the phone

The easiest way is to build the session on the computer and take it to the
phone:

- **Export the session as an `.ltset`** and open it with **Import session** on
  the start screen. On a modest phone, **Optimized** mode carries the audio
  already prepared and the session opens without waiting. More in
  [System requirements](/docs/system-requirements/#making-a-big-session-load-faster).
- **Through Google Drive**: export to Drive from the computer and, on the
  phone, **Import session → Google Drive**. The files are in your own Drive,
  not on any LibreTracks server. The account has to be connected on the phone
  too (see [The cloud](/docs/integration-ecosystem/#the-cloud-your-google-drive)).
