The **Remote** is LibreTracks in the browser of another device: a tablet on
the music stand, the drummer's phone, the music director's. There is nothing
to install. It controls the same session you have open on the computer: what
you press on the Remote happens on the computer, and what happens on the
computer shows on the Remote.

The Remote **does not play audio**. The sound always comes from the computer;
the Remote is only a controller.

## Connecting

1. In LibreTracks, press **Remote** in the side bar.
2. The computer and the tablet have to be on the **same network** (the same
   Wi‑Fi, or the computer wired to the same router).
3. On the tablet, **scan the QR code** with the camera, or type one of the two
   addresses into the browser.

![The Remote connection panel on the computer](/guide/desktop-en/remote-modal.png)

- **IP URL** works on any device.
- **Hostname URL (.local)** is easier to remember and does not change if the
  router gives the computer a different IP, but not every device understands
  it: if it does not open, use the IP one.

You can connect **several devices at once**, each with its own screen layout.
LibreTracks has to stay open on the computer while you use the Remote.

:::tip[If it will not connect]
Check that both are on the same network and that the Wi‑Fi is not a
"guest" network (those usually isolate devices from each other). On Windows,
if the firewall is blocking the Remote, the panel itself says so, with an
**Allow through the firewall** button (Windows asks for administrator
confirmation).
:::

On a **phone**, the Remote is meant to be used **in portrait**. If you open it
in landscape it asks you to rotate, with a **Use it in landscape anyway**
option. On a tablet it works both ways.

## The Controls tab

It is the first thing you see. This is how LibreTracks lays it out on a tablet:

![The Remote's Controls tab on a tablet](/guide/remote-en/remote-controls.png)

<Legend>

1. **Tabs**: Controls, Mixer and Tools. You can change them, add more and
   rename them (see [Designing your tabs](#designing-your-tabs)).
2. **Status**: a green dot while playing, amber when paused and grey when
   stopped. If the connection to the computer drops it says **Connecting…**
   or **No connection** instead of showing the last thing it heard.
3. **Full screen**: hides this bar to give the controls all the room (on
   Android it also asks the browser for full screen). A pill with the current
   tab stays: tap it to switch tabs or to bring the bar back.
4. **Edit layout**: opens the editor to move, add or remove blocks. On a
   phone it is just the pencil.
5. **Size**: **A−** and **A+** make the whole Remote bigger or smaller (four
   levels), without changing where anything is. Useful when you look at it
   from a distance. On a phone they live in the editor's bar.
6. **Readouts**: time, bar and beat, BPM, time signature and the song that is
   playing.
7. **Transport**: Play, Pause, Stop, **Fade out** (fades the song out and
   stops; tap it again to stop at once; see
   [Fade out and stop](/docs/interface/main-screen/#fade-out-and-stop)), and
   the **Click** and voice **Guide** switches. Play turns green while playing
   and Pause amber while paused.
8. **Timeline** of the song, with its markers and the cursor. Drag to move
   around. The button in the corner turns **Tap to seek** on: with it on, a tap
   on the timeline moves the cursor there.
9. **Live keys**: **VAMP** turns it on and off. **Vamp mode** (repeat the
   current section or a number of bars), **Jump** (when a jump to a marker
   happens: **Immediate**, after a few **Bars** or at the **Next** marker) and
   **Song** (when and how you move to another song: right away, after a few
   bars, at the next marker or at the end, with a **Clean cut** or a **Fade
   out**) show their value and open their options when tapped. While a jump is
   waiting, the Jump key becomes **Cancel jump** and says where it was going.
10. The setlist's **songs**, with their key if they have one. Tap one to
    select it.
11. **Transpose** changes the key of the selected song in semitones.
12. **Jump to Song**: pick the song in its list and jump to it with the
    transition you have set.
13. **Hide markers…**: enters a mode where hidden markers show too; a tap
    hides or shows a marker on this device (never jumping, and without
    removing it from the session). **Done** leaves, and the button counts how
    many are hidden.
14. The song's **markers**. Tap one to jump to it according to Jump. The
    section that is playing lights up in its colour with a **NOW** tag and a
    bar that fills up until the next one; the one that will play next is
    tagged **NEXT**. **Cues** show with a dashed border, so the band sees them
    coming.

</Legend>

Each key's options open in a floating panel above the layout; it closes by
tapping outside, with its `×` or with `Escape`.

Jumps, Vamp and transitions work as on the computer: the details are in
[Live control](/docs/live-control-flow/).

## The Mixer tab

![The Remote's Mixer tab](/guide/remote-en/remote-mixer.png)

<Legend>

1. **Active song only**: shows only the tracks that play in the current song.
   Off, they all show.
2. **Song master**: the volume of the selected song (the same as on the
   computer). It does not affect the click or the guide.
3. **Track**: its name, in its colour (folders stand out by theirs).
4. **Pan**: left, center or right. The value above the fader is also the
   centre button; a double tap on the fader does the same.
5. **Volume** in dB, with its meter.
6. **M** (mute), **S** (solo) and **T** (whether the track follows the
   [transposition](/docs/pitch-and-warp/)).

</Legend>

**Folders** have their own strip: its fader moves every track inside them.

The strips scroll sideways with your finger when they do not fit: about four
fit on a phone and seven or eight on a tablet.

## The Tools tab

![The Remote's Tools tab](/guide/remote-en/remote-tools.png)

<Legend>

1. **Metronome**: on/off, volume, output, accent, the sound and pitch of the
   accent and the beat, and the subdivision with its own sound and volume. See
   [Audio outputs and metronome](/docs/audio-routing-metronome/).
2. **Voice guide**: on/off, volume, output, language, how many bars ahead it
   announces and whether it counts in. See [Voice guide](/docs/voice-guide/).
3. **Ambient pads**: on/off, pack, output, key (or follow the song's),
   volume, fade in and out, and whether they stop with playback. See
   [Ambient pads](/docs/ambient-pads/).

</Legend>

Each panel **folds** away when you tap its title: folded, it keeps only its
on/off switch and frees the space. Each device remembers it.

Pad packs are downloaded and managed on the computer; the Remote shows the
ones you already have installed.

## Designing your tabs

Every musician needs different things: the drummer wants the click and big
markers; the director wants the jumps and the songs. With **Edit layout** you
build your own screen out of blocks (**widgets**) and spread it over as many
tabs as you like.

![The layout editor bar and a widget's controls](/guide/remote-en/remote-edit.png)

<Legend>

1. **Done**: keeps the changes and leaves the editor.
2. **Cancel**: leaves, undoing everything you changed since you came in.
3. **Free position** / **Push down**: how widgets are placed. In *Free
   position* they may overlap and nothing moves by itself. In *Push down*,
   dropping a widget on top of another pushes that one (and those below it)
   down to make room.
4. **Tab height**: makes the tab bar taller, to hit it with a finger.
5. **Export**, **Import** and **Reset layout** (see below).
6. Each **tab**: `‹ ›` reorder it, the pencil renames it and the `×` deletes
   it (the last one cannot be deleted).
7. **+ Tab**: adds an empty tab.
8. The widget's **title bar**: drag it to move the widget.
9. **Width** (`↔`) and **height** (`↕`) with buttons, and **Remove** to delete
   the widget.
10. **Corner**: drag it to resize with your finger.

</Legend>

When you enter the editor, the **widget palette** opens at the bottom. Drag a
widget to its place on the grid (you will see the space it is going to take)
or tap it to add it at the end. **Hide widgets** puts the palette away if it is
in the way; **Show widgets** opens it again.

![The widget palette](/guide/remote-en/remote-palette.png)

They are grouped like this:

- **Information**: the readouts together or one by one (time, bar/beat, BPM,
  time signature, song name, project name).
- **Transport**: the whole transport or each button on its own (Play, Pause,
  Stop, Fade out, Click, Guide), the timeline and **Black**, which blacks out the
  [video](/docs/tasks/video/) output (the projector) and brings it back with
  another tap.
- **Live control**: the whole control deck or its parts (Vamp, Jump config,
  Song transition, Song and transpose), the Jump to song button, the marker
  grid, and readouts for the band: **next section**, **next song**, **key**,
  progress bars **until the next section or song** and countdowns **in
  seconds or bars**.
- **Songs**: the **compact song view**, with its clips (the active song or
  **all** of them, in one row that scrolls sideways). Its play button starts
  the song, or jumps to it with the configured transition if another one is
  playing.
- **Lyrics & chords**: the lyrics of the song playing, following it line by
  line, with buttons for the size and to hide the chords. Lyrics are added
  from the computer's [Live View](/docs/live-view/#lyrics-and-chords).
- **Mixer**: the whole mixer or its parts (song filter, song master, faders).
- **Tools**: metronome, voice guide and pads.
- **Layout**: **titles** and **notes** with your own text, **blank space**,
  **separators** (line, dashed or space only) and **groups**. Drop a widget
  inside a group (its frame lights up) and it moves with it; drag it out to
  separate it. Removing a group does not remove what is inside.

Each widget adapts to its size: if you make it small, long lists (markers,
faders) scroll inside it instead of shrinking until they cannot be read.

### Where your layout is saved

The layout is saved **in that browser on that device**. Another tablet, or
another browser on the same one, starts with the default layout, adapted to
its size (phone, tablet or large screen).

- **Export** downloads your layout as a `.json` file.
- **Import** loads an exported `.json`, for example on another musician's
  tablet or a new device.
- **Reset layout** goes back to the default layout for that device.

If you clear the browser's data, the layout is lost: export a copy once it is
ready.

## Tips for the show

- Design the screen **on the same device and in the same orientation** you
  will use on stage, and at the same size level.
- Put the essentials (play, markers, jumps) where you do not need to scroll.
- If you can, wire the computer to the router and use a dedicated Wi‑Fi for
  the tablets. A crowded Wi‑Fi delays taps.
- Turn off the tablet's auto-lock during the show.
