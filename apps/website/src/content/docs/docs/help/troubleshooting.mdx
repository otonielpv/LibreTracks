First of all: **update to the latest version**. Many of these problems are
already fixed, and LibreTracks tells you when a new one is out.

## Installing and opening

### Windows says "Windows protected your PC" when installing

That is Windows SmartScreen, which warns about installers it does not know
yet. Click **More info** and then **Run anyway**. Only download LibreTracks
from [the download page](/download/) or from GitHub.

### On Windows the app closes a few seconds after opening

It is almost always an **ASIO driver** that fails when it is queried (it
happens with ASIO4ALL and with drivers for interfaces that are not
connected). Current versions detect it and hide that driver from the list
instead of closing. If it still happens with the latest version, uninstall the
ASIO drivers you do not use and send us the log (see [Getting help](#getting-help)).

### On Linux the window is blank (white)

It is a known bug in WebKitGTK, the system's web engine, with NVIDIA drivers
and on immutable distributions (Bazzite, Silverblue, SteamOS). LibreTracks
already applies the fix at startup. On immutable distributions, use the
**AppImage** (or a *distrobox* container) instead of installing the `.rpm` on
top of the system.

### macOS: it will not start on my Mac

LibreTracks needs **macOS 12 Monterey** or newer. See
[System requirements](/docs/system-requirements/).

## Sound

### Nothing plays

Check, in this order:

1. **Settings → Audio**: the right **output device**, with its outputs
   enabled.
2. The track's **output** (or its folder's): Master or another output that is
   actually connected to something. See [Audio outputs and metronome](/docs/audio-routing-metronome/).
3. **Mute** and **solo** on tracks and folders, and the **song's master**.
4. That the audio is not in the **missing files** list (see below).

### "No audio output — reconnecting…" appears

The audio interface stopped responding (it was unplugged, the driver hung or
Windows switched it). Playback **keeps running** and the sound comes back by
itself when the device responds again. If it does not, check the cable and
the output in Settings → Audio.

### "The app was idle" appears

If the app has gone unused for a good while (or the computer went to sleep),
press **Resume** to wake the audio up. It does not start playback: everything
stays as you left it.

### Clicks, or the audio sounds sped up

The **buffer size** is too small for your computer or audio system. Raise it
in **Settings → Audio** (512 is a good starting point). On Windows, use
**ASIO** if your interface has it, or **WASAPI**; with **DirectSound** a small
buffer gives exactly those symptoms. See [Settings](/docs/interface/settings/).

### When I press Play, some tracks are silent at first and then come in

Their audio is still being **prepared** (you will see "Preparing audio...").
Playback does not wait: the tracks come in by themselves as soon as they are
ready. On a modest computer, or with the audio on a slow drive, the first time
can take a moment; the next time is immediate. An **SSD** helps a lot.

### When I transpose, the sound arrives slightly late

Real‑time pitch shifting adds a small delay (around a tenth of a second). If
timing is critical, prepare the song already in the right key in your DAW. See
[Pitch, warp and the T button](/docs/pitch-and-warp/).

## Files and sessions

### "Missing media files" appears

LibreTracks plays your audio **from wherever it is**, without copying it. If
you moved, renamed or deleted a file, its tracks go silent. Click the notice
to open **Missing files**: if LibreTracks finds the file in another folder it
offers **Use the one in…**, and otherwise **Browse…** lets you locate it. To
avoid this when changing computers, export the session in **Full** mode; see
[Export, import and share](/docs/integration-ecosystem/).

### After updating, the session takes longer to open

Some updates change how audio is prepared, and the first time LibreTracks
rebuilds its cache. The next openings are fast again.

### The audio cache takes up a lot of space

In **Settings**, the **Audio cache** section shows where it is and how big it
is, and lets you set a maximum size, move it to another drive or clear it.
Clearing it does not delete anything of yours: it is rebuilt when needed.

## Remote

### The phone or tablet will not connect

- The computer and the device have to be on the **same network**. **Guest**
  Wi‑Fi networks usually isolate devices from each other.
- On Windows, if the firewall blocks the Remote, the **Remote** panel itself
  says so, with an **Allow through the firewall** button.
- If the **.local** address does not open, use the **IP URL**.

See [Custom Remote](/docs/remote-control/).

## Video

- **"Video unavailable"** on Linux: install your distribution's mpv library
  (`libmpv2` or `mpv-libs`).
- **The projector does not show up**: connect it and press **Refresh** in
  Settings → Video, or open the **Wizard…**.
- **The picture lags behind the music**: use **Calibrate…**.

See [Video on the projector](/docs/tasks/video/).

## Phone

- **Importing a big session is very slow** on a modest phone: that is the
  speed of its storage. Export it from the computer in **Optimized** mode,
  which opens without preparing the audio, or split it into smaller sessions.
- **No room to import**: you need twice the package size free, plus a margin.
  LibreTracks checks before it starts.
- **Android: "file extension is not valid" when importing a WAV** picked from
  some file apps: it is fixed; update the app from Google Play.

More in [LibreTracks on Mobile](/docs/mobile/) and in
[System requirements](/docs/system-requirements/).

## Getting help

If your problem is not here, tell us in the
[Facebook group](https://www.facebook.com/groups/1795788251804551), on
[Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/LibreTracks/) or by opening an issue on
[GitHub](https://github.com/otonielpv/LibreTracks/issues).

Attaching the **error log** helps a lot: in **Settings → Diagnostics**, press
**Copy error log** and paste it into your message, or **Open logs folder** to
send the files. Tell us your system, your audio interface and what you were
doing when it happened.
